# Bedrock Panel v0.9.6-beta.4 — Linux beta (x64)

Bedrock Panel runs on Linux with the DK-QUAKE: Software mode, Panel mode on the 1920×480 display,
the knob and touchscreen, the tile grids, the editor, dashboards, and every platform-neutral app.
This is a **beta for testers**. Everything below is verified on Ubuntu 26.04 with KDE Plasma 6.6 on
Wayland, PipeWire, and a real DK-QUAKE.

## What changed since beta.3

**Speech, and everything that needed it.** beta.3 could not speak or listen without a server of your
own. This build does both on the machine in front of you, with nothing to install and no GPU.

- **Speaking and listening are built in.** Editor → **Settings → TTS/STT**: pick a voice, pick a
  listening language, press **Set up** on each. About 140 MB downloads once and then works offline.
  The two halves are separate downloads — take one, the other, or both.
- **Meeting transcription runs on this computer**, with named speakers. Editor →
  **Settings → Meetings** → Engine → *Built-in speech on this computer*.
- **LucidType dictation** and the **AI Voice** apps use the built-in engine automatically.

Nothing else changed. Monitor mode, global hotkeys, macros and the packaging are beta.3's.

**Download:** `bedrock-panel_amd64.deb` for Debian and Ubuntu, or `bedrock-panel-x86_64.AppImage`
for anything else. Windows and macOS builds are unchanged in this beta (v0.9.5 remains current).

## Setting up speech

1. **Settings → TTS/STT.** Under **Voice**, choose one and press **Set up** (about 85 MB). Under
   **Listening**, choose a language and press **Set up** (about 91 MB, which includes the models that
   tell speakers apart in meetings).
2. That is all. There is no server to run and no port to enter.

They are real Wyoming servers on 127.0.0.1:10200 and :10300, so anything else on the machine that
speaks that protocol — Home Assistant, for one — can use them too. **If you already run your own
Piper or Whisper on those ports, yours wins**: Bedrock Panel notices the port is taken and uses what
is there.

Everything offered is free of restrictions. The voices are public domain or CC0, the recognition
model is MIT, and the speaker models are MIT and Apache-2.0.

## Meeting transcription

Set Engine to *Built-in speech on this computer* under **Settings → Meetings**.

A meeting has two problems in it and this handles them separately. You are told apart from the call
by **audio channel** — recordings are stereo, your microphone on one side and the call on the other
— so that half cannot be got wrong. The people on the call are told apart by **voice**.

**Enroll someone once and their name appears in every later transcript.** Under **Known voices**,
pick a WAV of that person talking: 45 seconds or more, recorded the way your meetings actually are,
since a voice learned through a headset sounds different through a conference speaker. Enrolling is
optional — unenrolled voices are still separated, just numbered *Speaker A*, *Speaker B*.

Voice profiles are ordinary `.npy` files named after the person, the same format tts-sst and the
Python meeting-diarizer write, so **a profile folder copies between machines** and nobody has to be
enrolled twice. They live in `~/.config/bedrock-panel/speech/speakers`.

The identification threshold, the attendee penalty and the clustering constants are the same numbers
tts-sst uses, so a threshold tuned there is still right here.

## What to try

- **Say something with a voice app** and hear it answer, with no server configured anywhere.
- **Dictate with LucidType** and watch it transcribe locally.
- **Transcribe a real meeting** with two or more people on the call, and check that they come out as
  separate speakers. Enroll one of them and run it again.
- **Interrupt the assistant mid-sentence** in an AI Voice app; it should cut off cleanly and carry on.
- Everything from beta.3: Monitor mode, global hotkeys, macros and media keys.

## Known limits in this beta

- **Listening is English only.** Other languages mean pointing STT at your own Wyoming server. The
  voices offered are English, US and UK.
- **A person must be enrolled before they can be named.** Until then they are numbered.
- **Someone who speaks for less than five seconds in a whole meeting is not reported as a
  participant.** Their words fall back to the generic label rather than inventing a person out of
  crosstalk.
- **Transcription is built for meetings recorded by Bedrock Panel** — 16 kHz stereo with your mic on
  the left. Another recorder's file is refused rather than split down the middle and guessed at.
- **Real-name accuracy depends on your enrollment clip.** Short or unrepresentative audio produces a
  profile that matches nobody, including its owner.
- **A display scaled to anything other than 100% is untested** with Monitor mode.
- **Reserved Display**, **follow the focused app** and **Teams window control** are not available;
  Wayland does not allow moving other applications' windows.
- **Now playing and system volume** are still not wired up, so the Music page is blank and the
  meeting console's output rail shows a dash. The media *keys* work.
- **Outlook desktop meeting info** is Windows-only; use the Microsoft 365 source.
- **AppImage needs FUSE 2** — install `libfuse2t64` or use the `.deb`.
- x64 only. No arm64 build yet.
