# Bedrock Panel v0.9.6-beta.18 — Linux beta (x64)

Bedrock Panel runs on Linux with the DK-QUAKE: Software mode, Panel mode on the 1920×480 display,
the knob and touchscreen, the tile grids, the editor, dashboards, and every platform-neutral app.
This is a **beta for testers**. Everything below is verified on Ubuntu 26.04 with KDE Plasma 6.6 on
Wayland, PipeWire, and a real DK-QUAKE.

## What changed since beta.17

**The Music page works, and this time the cause is known rather than guessed at.**

The panel was never failing to read MPRIS. It was being refused, by AppArmor, with an error nobody
was printing:

```
org.freedesktop.DBus.Error.AccessDenied
An AppArmor policy prevents this sender from sending this message to this recipient;
  sender label="bedrock-panel (unconfined)"
  destination="org.mpris.MediaPlayer2.firefox.instance_1_324"
  recipient label="snap.firefox.firefox (enforce)"
```

Two things on Ubuntu collide. The installer puts an AppArmor profile on this app, because since
Ubuntu 24.04 an application needs one before its sandbox may create user namespaces. That profile
permits everything; Ubuntu's own profiles for Chrome and VS Code carry a comment saying they exist
"only to give the application a name instead of having the label unconfined". And a snap-packaged
player accepts media traffic only from senders labelled `unconfined` or `plasmashell` — a rule
written into snapd's mpris interface. So the moment this app got a name, every snap refused to talk
to it. On Ubuntu that is Firefox, Chromium and Spotify: most of what anyone plays music with.

A process cannot drop its AppArmor label except across an exec, and the app needs its profile. So
when the app finds itself labelled, the media reading now happens one process out, in a child that
sheds the label on its way in. It is the same code, running under the app's own binary, so there is
nothing extra to install. Where there is no AppArmor label — every other distribution — nothing
changes and no child is started.

This also explains the six earlier builds. The Python helper that read MPRIS until beta.16 was
inheriting the same label and being refused in exactly the same way. It was not a broken pipe; the
helper was being told no and had nothing to say.

## What to try

- **Play something in Firefox, Chromium or Spotify** and open the Music page. Track, artist, album,
  progress and cover art should all appear, and follow a track change within a second.
- **Play, pause, next and previous** from the panel, including with two players open. The buttons
  should drive the player being shown.
- **Start the music before opening the panel.** Something already playing should appear immediately.
- **A non-snap player** (VLC, Elisa, mpv from your distribution's packages) should behave the same.

Firefox withdraws its media player entirely when playback stops, so an empty Music page after
pausing in Firefox is correct rather than a regression.

## Everything else in this beta run

Built-in speaking and listening with no server, GPU or account. 90 voices in 36 languages, each one
public domain, CC0, CC BY or MIT, with a preview before you download. Meeting transcription with
named speakers and enrollment. LucidType dictation. The AI Voice apps, with barge-in. Live Translate.
The meeting console's output rail. Auto-record and the busy light when an application takes the
microphone. Monitor mode with the touchscreen as a pointer, global hotkeys, and macros.

## Known limits in this beta

- **A person must be enrolled before a meeting transcript can name them.** Until then they are
  separated and numbered, and someone who speaks for less than five seconds in the whole meeting is
  not reported as a participant.
- **A display scaled to anything other than 100% is untested** with Monitor mode.
- **Reserved Display**, **follow the focused app** and **reading a calendar on this computer** are
  not available and are greyed out with the reason; Wayland does not allow moving or watching other
  applications' windows. Use the Microsoft 365 calendar source, which works everywhere.
- **Outlook desktop meeting info** is Windows-only.
- **AppImage needs FUSE 2** — install `libfuse2t64` or use the `.deb`.
- x64 only. No arm64 build yet.

**Download:** `bedrock-panel_amd64.deb` for Debian and Ubuntu, or `bedrock-panel-x86_64.AppImage`
for anything else. Windows and macOS builds are unchanged in this beta (v0.9.5 remains current).
